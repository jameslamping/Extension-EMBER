>> Fire Reduction Parameters — 10 severity classes for SCF/SCRPPLE
>> ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
>> Columns are fractions (0.0–1.0) of each pool volatilized by a fire of a given
>> severity class (NECN v8.2 §2.34). The remainder of cohort wood/leaf killed by
>> fire is deposited into the dead-wood and dead-leaf pools, not lost.
>>
>> Severity scale used here (consistent with SCF/SCRPPLE 1–10):
>>   1–2  smoldering / very low surface fire
>>   3–4  low surface fire
>>   5–6  moderate surface fire, occasional passive crown
>>   7–8  active crown fire, high overstory mortality
>>   9–10 extreme stand-replacing fire with deep duff consumption
>>
>> Calibrated for PNW maritime/montane conifer forests (MORA, NOCA, OLYM).
>> Key ecological constraints encoded:
>>   - Fine litter consumes readily even at low severity (Ottmar 2014).
>>   - Coarse downed wood retains moisture; complete consumption is rare even
>>     in high-severity fire (Brown et al. 1985; Hyde et al. 2011).
>>   - Live cohort wood (boles) is largely converted to snags rather than
>>     volatilized; capped at 0.45 even at severity 10 (Reinhardt et al. 1997).
>>   - PNW O-horizons are moist and rarely fully consumed; capped at 0.85
>>     (Ottmar 2014; Prichard et al. 2017).
>>
>> General references:
>>   Brown, J.K., M.A. Marsden, K.C. Ryan, E.D. Reinhardt. 1985. Predicting
>>     duff and woody fuel consumed by prescribed fire in the Northern Rocky
>>     Mountains. USDA For. Serv. Res. Pap. INT-337.
>>   Reinhardt, E.D., R.E. Keane, J.K. Brown. 1997. First Order Fire Effects
>>     Model: FOFEM 4.0, User's Guide. USDA For. Serv. GTR INT-344.

>>     The combustion of sound and rotten coarse woody debris: a review.
